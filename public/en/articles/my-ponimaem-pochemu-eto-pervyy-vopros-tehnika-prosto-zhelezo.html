We understand why this is the first question: hardware is just metal, but holiday photos are an evening...

12 September 2026

We understand why this is the first question: hardware is just metal, but holiday photos are an evening on the promenade, a funny sunset and a backpack full of emotions. So let's be honest about when photos can still be recovered from an iPhone that won't turn on, and when the device is silent forever and it's not its fault, but ours.

First, the good news: most often the storage chip with the photos is alive. An iPhone usually won't turn on for three reasons: a deeply discharged battery, a system failure, or a faulty board. In the first two cases, the photos don't go anywhere — they sit quietly on the memory chip and wait for the phone to come back to life. A technician knows this, so they check the simplest things first: the battery and the system.

So the first thing to do at home is to put the phone on charge with an original charger for at least half an hour. If the battery is completely dead, the device may not respond immediately: first a small current will flow, then the screen will flash the Apple logo. Don't press buttons unnecessarily — a forced restart at this point can get in the way.

But if the phone stays black after charging, or an Apple logo appears and hangs, or a stripe appears on the screen — that's not for gadgets, that's for diagnostics. The board is a precise thing: corrosion, microcracks, a detached controller. Photos can usually still be saved, but the intervention must be careful and professional. One wrong move — and the data is gone forever.

Now the unpleasant part — when photos can no longer be saved. This happens if the phone had a serious liquid spill in the past, and the owner kept using it without drying it inside. Corrosion eats away the traces to the memory chip, and the data is physically destroyed. Here neither a technician nor the best lab can bring back even one photo.

And of course, the classic — backups. If iCloud sent a notification "storage full" and you swiped it away — that means there are no recent photos in the cloud. The holiday archive is only in the phone itself. So as soon as your beloved iPhone comes back to life — make a copy right away, don't put it off until the evening.

It works like this: an iPhone is a reliable thing, but not reinforced concrete. First, calm diagnostics: understand why it's silent. If it's the battery or the system — the photos are in no danger. If it's the board — it's time to save them properly. And the best protection against "everything is lost" is a simple backup, which has never hurt any holiday.
