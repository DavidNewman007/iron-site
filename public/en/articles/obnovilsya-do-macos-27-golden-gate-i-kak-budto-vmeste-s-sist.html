Updated to macOS 27 Golden Gate — and it feels like a second user came along with the system, one who eats the battery.

11 November 2026

Updated to macOS 27 Golden Gate — and it feels like a second user came along with the system, one who eats the battery. Familiar? After major updates this happens: for the first few days the system rebuilds indexes, pulls photos to the cloud and quietly shuffles hundreds of small processes. All of it drains the charge before your eyes.

The first thing worth doing is simply waiting a couple of days. If the MacBook is actively chewing on something in the background, battery life can dip temporarily, and that is no reason to run out for a new battery. Yes, we have lived through plenty of updates after which the laptop barely made it to lunch, and a week later it was back to lasting all day. That is normal.

Next — look at who exactly is sipping the charge. Open Activity Monitor, the Energy tab. There you can see which process is the greediest: the spotlight indexer, mail, iCloud sync. If some process is grinding away non-stop, that is a typical software glitch, not a verdict on the battery.

Now about real wear. In System Settings there is a section with the battery condition and the recharge cycle count. If the cycles have piled up and the condition says service is required, then the update is not to blame here. macOS 27 simply leaned on an old battery, and it showed its age. So the coincidence of both events is often mistaken for a cause.

What to do about it? With a software glitch, a rollback or waiting for a fresh patch helps; with wear, only a battery replacement. People bring such machines to our workshop, and the first thing we do is run our own battery diagnostics: we check capacity, the cycle counter and behaviour under load. It happens that a person is already mentally prepared to replace the battery, when in fact macOS simply cannot settle down.

So, in order of priority: waited a couple of days — better? Then the battery is alive and it was the system acting up. Not better, and capacity has noticeably dropped? Check Activity Monitor, then the battery condition. A computer will not live longer if you keep telling it to hang on another year 🗿
