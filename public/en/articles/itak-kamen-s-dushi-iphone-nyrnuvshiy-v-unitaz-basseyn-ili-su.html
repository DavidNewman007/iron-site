So, a weight off the mind: an iPhone that has taken a dive into the toilet, pool or snowdrift is not always a one-way ticket...

23 August 2026

So, a weight off the mind: an iPhone that has taken a dive into the toilet, pool or snowdrift is not always a one-way ticket to a new device. It is time to debunk the myth that Apple tech dies instantly from water. Yes, contact oxidation is a sneaky business, but between 'got wet' and 'dead forever' there are often a few hours.

The main enemy after a spill is not the water itself, but your attempts to play the hero. Pressing buttons, plugging in a charger and especially drying with a hairdryer spread moisture across the board, turning a puddle on a chip into a full-blown short circuit. Water inside a modern case is like an uninvited guest: as long as you do not touch it, it sits quietly, but the moment you apply power, corrosion begins.

Proper first aid is boring: switch it off, wipe it, do not shake it and do not blow on it. No rice! The grains give off starchy dust that clogs connectors worse than sand. The ideal scenario is to put the device in a dry place and just let it sit while you head to the technicians.

Because visually the phone can look perfectly alive: the screen lights up, there is sound. But inside, on the motherboard, an irreversible (for now) oxidation process of the traces has already started. It is exactly these 'quiet ones' that most often end up on our bench, with a diagnosis of 'wet but seems to work'. Sometimes they can be brought back to life in a few hours of careful ultrasonic cleaning.

There are also truly sad cases: a phone that has lain on the seabed or been drenched in beer six months ago. That is a lottery. Even if it shows no signs of life, it is too early to give up. Practice shows that after a full teardown and restoration of the power circuits, many 'drowned ones' return to their owners, though with a lost warranty and a drained battery that will most likely also need replacing.

The moral is simple: if your Apple gadget suddenly decides to go for a swim, do not perform shamanic dances with a tambourine and warm air. Our hero in armour is already waiting in the workshop. While you carry the phone to the service centre, he will calmly and without panic take it apart, dry it and check every trace under the microscope. After all, for a true technician, water is not a tragedy, but just a technical task.
