There's a habit that makes the hair stand up on end for anyone who has ever handled someone else's iPhone for repair:…

18 November 2026

There's a habit that makes the hair stand up on end for anyone who has ever handled someone else's iPhone for repair: the person hands in the device, and when asked about the password for the local backup, just shrugs. "I never made one." Or they did, but the password long ago dissolved in memory along with the PIN code for a bank card from ten years back.

Meanwhile, an encrypted local backup is not the whim of a paranoid person. It contains what an ordinary backup does not: saved passwords, Wi-Fi settings, browser history, health data and the call log. In essence, it is a snapshot of your digital life minus Face ID, Touch ID and the device passcode — Apple fundamentally does not store those in a backup.

The elegance of it is that by default the backup is not encrypted. Until you enable the Encrypt local backup option and come up with a password, all that valuable content flies onto the computer in plain text. Once you enable it, the system encrypts all subsequent backups — and starts demanding the password on every restore. Lost the password? You still can't restore the old backup. You can only forget about it and make a new one, with a new password.

Why does this come up specifically before a repair? Because cleaning, diagnostics, replacing the battery or the display are always a lottery when it comes to the firmware. No one will guarantee that you won't be asked to restore data from a backup. And if that moment comes and you don't remember the backup password, there will be two options: hope for iCloud or start life with a clean slate.

So before visiting the service centre — five minutes of talking to yourself. Have I been making encrypted backups? Where do I keep the passwords for them? If the password is written on a sticker stuck to the monitor, that's also an option, but better in a password manager, which you have, in fact, encrypted in that very backup. The irony here is thick as syrup.

Technicians, by the way, understand perfectly well when a client furrows their brow and honestly admits that the password is written down somewhere. We'll tell you where to look and what to do. The main thing is not to leave this question for later. Later sometimes arrives at the most inconvenient moment.
