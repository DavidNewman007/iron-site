Truth or myth: an old MacBook belongs only in a desk drawer

23 August 2026

🤔

Today we take apart a legend that has ruined a lot of good hardware. It goes like this: “If your MacBook is old and can’t handle everything anymore — there’s no hope, you can only use it through pain, and honestly, it’s easier to just get a new one.”

This is a **myth**, and a rather cruel one at that, when it comes to your loyal laptop 🥲.

In reality, the “Apple veteran” has plenty of reserve strength. Nobody is stopping you from taking away its crown as the “main machine” and sending it off to a well-earned fight: email, messengers, cloud documents, and “just to look at some code while the main one is busy.” The hardware is intact! And to make all of that fly rather than try to boil over from a single browser tab, it’s enough to dust off the system.

The first candidate for retirement is everything that has been hanging in the background for years and eating resources. We clear out the obvious junk (ten copies of updates and memes from 2018), remove the extras from startup. The second step is maintenance: a new battery and dust prevention turn a “retiree’s dream” into a collected assistant. And a mechanical clean of the old drive is like trying to shake hands with a person who has just seen that sluggishness in the mirror and decided to become stronger — that’s how luxurious the effect is.

In short, giving a veteran a second chance is not exactly rocket science. The main thing is the right head on your shoulders and hands that know how to breathe life into hardware. And if you want that second wind to open up reliably and with a guarantee — well, you know where your hands will be supported 🤝
