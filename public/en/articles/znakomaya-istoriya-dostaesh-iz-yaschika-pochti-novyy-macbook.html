A familiar story: you take a nearly new MacBook Pro out of a drawer, let out a nostalgic sigh — and it won't even turn on.

24 August 2026

A familiar story: you take a nearly new MacBook Pro out of a drawer, let out a nostalgic sigh — and it won't even turn on. Not because it's outdated. But because the battery doesn't like sitting idle. A year, two, three — and suddenly a "practically unused" laptop behaves like a retiree coming out of winter hibernation.

Generally speaking, a battery is a finicky thing. It needs to wake up regularly, discharge, and fill back up. A full zero over a long period is the worst-case scenario. But the good news is that the laptop itself is often in perfect shape: the processor is lively, the screen is fresh, the keyboard is pristine. For a ten-year-old dreaming of their first computer, such hardware is out of this world.

It's a shame to write off hardware just because of a dead battery. Especially when the rest of the internals can still surprise with speed. Our technician in armour looks at such "resurrections" with professional enthusiasm: sometimes all it takes is giving the battery a new life — and the veteran is back in action.
