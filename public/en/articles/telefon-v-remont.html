Phone in for repair

13 November 2026

Passwords stay with you

There's more in a phone than a cracked screen. There's your bank, your messages and family photos. So before visiting a repair shop it's worth sorting out two separate things: preserving what matters and understanding what access the technician will actually need. A note with the code written on the case doesn't solve this.

Start with a backup if the phone works. You can save it to iCloud or on your own computer; a local backup that includes passwords and Health data needs encryption enabled. Keep that backup's password separately: without it the backup can't be restored. Check the date of the last completed backup. And separately, wait for photo syncing to finish if you use iCloud Photos: that's a different mechanism, and those images aren't included in the daily backup.

Don't hand over your Apple Account password, verification codes or unlock code in messages, over the phone or on a piece of paper. Apple explicitly recommends not sharing this information with others. If signing in is needed to check a software issue, the owner enters the details themselves and stays nearby while the technician works on the unlocked device. That's a useful boundary when dealing with an independent shop too.

Access to the phone and access to the whole account are different things. Describe the fault in advance: when it happens, after which action, whether it repeats with a different cable. At check-in, ask what can be tested without unlocking and what will require your involvement. Promising that any repair can be fully tested on a locked device would be wrong: how much can be checked depends on the fault.

Don't turn off protection or erase the device just "to be safe". Apple has a separate preparation for service, including steps involving Find My and Wallet; the procedure depends on the system version and how you're getting service. It isn't a universal instruction for any repair shop. For diagnostics at an independent shop, agree on the necessary steps first. If the phone won't turn on, do only what you can and say straight away that the data matters and there's no backup.

Hand over the device with a clear agreement: what we're checking, which steps require your involvement and whether the data needs backing up first. And when collecting it, check the result together with the technician. At IRON SERVICE you can start by describing the problem in the order bot: that way the main details are to hand before your visit.

Sources: Apple Support — preparation for service (support.apple.com/en-ie/109519), what iCloud backup includes (support.apple.com/en-us/108770), encrypted backups (support.apple.com/en-sg/108353).
