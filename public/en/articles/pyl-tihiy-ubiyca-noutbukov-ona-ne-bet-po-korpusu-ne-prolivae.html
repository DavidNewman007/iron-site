Dust is the quiet killer of laptops

14 August 2026

It does not hit the case, it does not spill onto the keyboard and it does not fall from a height. It simply accumulates. Month after month it settles invisibly on the fan blades, clogs the radiators and turns thin air channels into dense felt. A MacBook starts to breathe with more and more effort — and you can hear it.

The first warning is noise. The fan spins up to its limit with nothing but a browser open, as though a miniature vacuum cleaner has moved in. The second is heat. The bottom panel gets warm enough to be uncomfortable on your lap, and even the keys feel warm. But the most dangerous sign is throttling. The processor drops its clock speed to avoid cooking itself, and a powerful MacBook Pro suddenly starts thinking more slowly than an ageing Air.

Inside, a real struggle is going on. The processor is desperately trying to cool down, the fan is howling at full speed, and the thermal paste between the chip and the heatsink no longer works the way it did in the first year. The aluminium body, which Apple made part of the cooling system, turns into a hotplate. Every overheat shortens the life of the battery, the chips and even the SSD. It is slow but steady wear at the cellular level of the machine.

A curious fact: 70–80% of the dust inside a laptop is dead human skin cells. The same ones we leave on bedding, sofas and clothes. That is exactly why a laptop that “sleeps” in a soft bed or works on a woollen blanket clogs up several times faster: the fabric acts as a filter that traps particles, and the air intakes pull them inside without pause.

A careful clean of the cooling system is not cosmetics — it gives the laptop back several years of life. Opening the case, removing the fans, clearing the radiators, renewing the thermal interface: the sequence takes knowledge and the right tools. One wrong move and the contacts on the board say goodbye without warning. So sometimes the best investment in your machine is handing it to people who have undone those screws a thousand times 🔧
