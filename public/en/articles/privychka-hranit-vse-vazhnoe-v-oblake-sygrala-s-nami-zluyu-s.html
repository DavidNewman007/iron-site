The habit of keeping everything important in the cloud played a nasty trick on us

7 September 2026

Photos, videos, work files — we trust them to invisible servers and only remember about it when something happens to the phone. But sometimes what you need is not a status of “in the cloud”, but a physical copy. The kind you can touch, put in a desk drawer, or hand to someone close to you.

The good news is that the iPhone has long been able to work directly with ordinary external drives. You take a compatible flash drive or SSD, plug it into the phone — and from there everything is sorted without a Mac and without a computer at all. The Files app sees the drive like a regular folder and lets you move photos, videos and documents onto it. Simple, like moving things from one pocket to another.

The catch is that the standard tools are not enough for everyone. Some are fine with manual selection: pick what you need — copy it. But if you want to make a full copy all at once and not go through thousands of files one by one, you have to look for workarounds. For example, third-party apps that can pull whole layers of data off the phone in one tap. The same idea of “plugged in a drive — chose what to save — got a physical copy” is refined in those.

What to store, everyone decides for themselves, but there are things that hurt the most to lose. And here a physical drive is not a replacement for the cloud, but an airbag under the airbag. A phone can get drowned, smashed, or simply refuse to turn on at the worst possible moment. Recovering it afterwards is a solvable task, but pulling the data out if the only copy lived solely inside — sometimes no one can manage that.

So the rule is simple: important things should exist in at least two places, and at least one of them should not be the phone. The cloud is convenient. A flash drive is peace of mind. And if your phone is already blinking the Apple logo, won’t turn on, or hints that storage is about to run out, first save the data, then repair. Or do both at once: first a copy, then diagnostics — so there is somewhere to put everything you have accumulated.
