Updated to macOS 27 Golden Gate — and it's as if the laptop was swapped

5 November 2026

The battery now lasts half a day, even though just yesterday it easily made it to the evening. Sound familiar? Then don't rush to write off the battery: after major updates this happens, and it's far from always the hardware's fault.

First — why this happens at all. A fresh system is busy in the background for the first few days: recalculating indexes, processing the photo stream, pulling app updates. The processor runs at higher speeds, and consumption climbs before your eyes. This isn't a breakdown — it's a housewarming. Usually the bustle settles within two or three days, and battery life returns.

If a week has passed and the MacBook still holds noticeably less, it's worth digging deeper. Look in "System Settings" → "Battery": there you can see which programs are eating energy. Sometimes it's not the Mac itself but a single process at fault — some background helper that got off on the wrong foot after the update. A restart, cleaning up the login items, sometimes simply waiting for a fix from the developer solves the issue without any repair.

And then comes the fork in the road. If the system has settled down, background processes are asleep, and battery life has still dropped — it's time to check the battery itself. MacOS can show the charge cycle count and remaining capacity, and here's where to pay attention: if the numbers are close to the limit, then the update isn't to blame. It's just a coincidence. A major update is like a move: in the commotion, everything that was long overdue for fixing surfaces. Battery wear is from the same series.

People are already bringing such MacBooks to our service: they say they updated — and that's it, the battery died. We check based on the facts: for some, after diagnostics everything is fine, the system was simply still digesting the update. For others, the battery really was living out its last cycles, and Golden Gate merely highlighted the problem. Replacing the battery in that case brings back both battery life and peace of mind. The more precisely the cause is understood, the easier the fix. So first — pause and a light check, and only then panic and a suitcase to the technician.
