The question that comes up more often than 'will you really fix it in an hour', and which is harder to answer honestly than it seems

30 August 2026

The question that comes up more often than 'will you really fix it in an hour', and which is harder to answer honestly than it seems. Because the answer doesn't start with a word, but with a question: who exactly is this 'original' of yours?

Any part has a story. The 'factory' one sits in the device from the production line, passed quality control along with the whole unit, and knows it down to the last pixel. But an 'original' for replacement is another life: the same panel, the same idea, but assembled later, without factory guarantees, for a specific repair. It's like an autograph from your idol made ten years after the fame: the handwriting looks the same, but the energy is no longer there.

'Aftermarket' is a word everyone fears for no reason. The market is flooded with parts from factories that make exactly the same thing, just without the apple on the box. A good aftermarket part is invisible to the eye — brightness is fine, colours don't shift green at an angle, while a bad one gives itself away with a blue tint or acidic overexposure at minimum brightness. Not every eye can tell the difference, but fingers can: fingers always feel when the touchscreen has a mind of its own.

The truth is that screen 'originality' is not a yes/no split, but a ladder of quality. The real choice is not between 'factory' and 'fake', but between a part that will last noticeably longer without surprises and one that will start falling apart quickly. And that is a question not for the label, but for the person who puts it on.

A modern iPhone does not forgive indifference: the screen for it is not just glass on top, but half the experience. A mistake in selection — and Face ID loses part of its vision, True Tone rides off into the sunset, and auto-brightness starts panicking on a sunny day. A good display has to get along with the system, otherwise it's just pretty glass.

So when people ask us 'is it original', we honestly suggest not trusting the word, but looking at the result. Colours as close to factory as possible, response without hesitation, the frame sits with no gaps. If all that is in place, then what difference does it make what passport the panel has? What matters is not the brand, but how the screen behaves in your hands a month after the repair.

And yes, we've seen both options opened up. A good aftermarket part is not ashamed to be shown next to an original — and that, perhaps, is stronger than any promise.
