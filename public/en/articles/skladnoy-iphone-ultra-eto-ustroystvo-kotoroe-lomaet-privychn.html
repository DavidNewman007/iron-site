The foldable iPhone Ultra is a device that breaks the usual logic of diagnostics

13 November 2026

You used to drop your phone, pick it up, look at it: the glass is intact, the screen responds — and you carry on. That doesn't work with a flexible display. After a fall everything can look perfect on the outside, while inside there is already a microcrack in the ribbon cable or a deformation of the hinge that will show itself later as a black stripe at the fold.

The hinge is the most treacherous part of the design. It is engineered to withstand a great many folds, but a drop onto asphalt or awkward pressure in a bag strikes not the screen but the pins, bushings and seals inside the mechanism. The first sign that something is wrong is not a crunch but a changed opening angle. The phone no longer unfolds fully — that alone is enough for the flexible display to be working under constant tension.

That is why, after any serious drop, you should fold and unfold the device slowly, listening for evenness of travel. If you feel resistance at one point, as if a grain of sand has got between the links, that is a signal. Ordinary diagnostics run through the ribbon cables with a utility will only show what the controller sees, and it will not see problems with the geometry of the hinge.

Dust and moisture are a separate matter. Wherever the folding design opens up access, the seals wear out over time. A little play is normal for any mechanism, but it is precisely this that lets fine debris in — debris that would have had nowhere to get into a classic monolithic iPhone. When folded, the phone presses the screens against each other, and any crumb caught between the halves acts as a point of pressure on the matrix.

Many future Ultra owners hope a case will solve most of the problems. It will protect against scratches — yes. Against deformation from a drop on the edge — partly. But it will not save you from the internal mechanics. If the phone falls while half open, the impact load goes into the hinge at an angle it was not designed for, and this is exactly the case where "it works, so it's fine" does not work.

What should you do? After buying a foldable iPhone, it is worth remembering the main rule: after any drop, even from a small height, even onto something soft, carefully check the folding geometry. Fold it slowly, open it slowly, run a finger along the hinge's support line, compare the halves against the light. If in doubt, it is better to have it checked an extra time on professional equipment than to end up needing a flexible screen replacement because a drifting axis was not noticed in time. Delaying diagnostics on a foldable iPhone costs noticeably more.
