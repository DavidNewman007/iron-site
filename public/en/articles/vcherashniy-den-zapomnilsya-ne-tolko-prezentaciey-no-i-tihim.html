Yesterday was memorable not only for the presentation, but also for the quiet launch of the Muse app from Meta AI...

9 September 2026

Yesterday was memorable not only for the presentation, but also for the quiet launch of the Muse app from Meta AI for iPhone. In short: this is no longer a chatbot waiting for your command, but a personal agent that clears out digital clutter on its own. Imagine you have hired a capable assistant who does not ask unnecessary questions, but simply gets on with it. 🤖

The main difference between Muse and the usual assistants is autonomy. You can feed it a whole task, from the wording to the action plan, and it will break it down into steps, find the necessary data, write replies or prepare documents. It is like the difference between a taxi driver who waits for directions and a navigator who has already plotted the route and is guiding you along it.

In appearance, Muse resembles other modern assistants in its interface, but inside it is a paradigm shift: you do not dictate every word, you set a goal and check the result. Handy for routine tasks: putting together a report, booking a meeting, comparing prices or sketching out a travel plan. The app is already available in the App Store — you can give it a try.

Of course, such free handling of personal data raises security questions. Meta assures that the agent operates within the permissions granted, but anyone who has used "smart" features knows: trust, but verify. In our line of work, we also often see the consequences of overly bold experiments with data — hardware does not like that. 🔧

The key question is not whether the neural network can carry out tasks. It can. The question is how smoothly this digital assistant will fit into your real life, where everything comes down not to algorithms, but to plain compatibility and the condition of the device. Fresh software requires fresh hardware.

If Muse is as good as they claim, then soon we will be spending less time in apps and more time resting. But for the assistant not to stall halfway, the iPhone itself has to be in good shape. And if something does go wrong — we know where to find our man in armour. Unlike the neural network, he works with hardware directly. 😉
