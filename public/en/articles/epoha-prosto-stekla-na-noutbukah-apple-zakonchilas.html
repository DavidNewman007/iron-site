The era of "just glass" on Apple laptops is over

31 October 2026

For the first time in the company's history, the MacBook Pro has got a touchscreen — now you can not only sadly run a cloth over the display, but also poke it with your finger. It looks like a breakthrough, until you remember the main rule of touch technology: the glass has become smarter, which means more expensive and more fragile to repair.

Previously, a damaged MacBook screen often came down to replacing the top lid or the glass assembly with the matrix. That is expensive, but the logic of the repair was clear. Now Apple has used a design familiar to every iPhone owner: the OLED panel and the digitizer that reads touches are combined into one inseparable unit. The corner of the table you have been dashing past with an open laptop for years has turned into a full-fledged enemy with a price list.

The main thing to understand: a touchscreen is not just a fashionable feature. It is an extra layer of electronics right under your fingers. Any impact now hits not just the picture, but the input system. A crack in one place — half the gestures stop responding. Touch not working — the laptop is left without a keyboard and trackpad, and that is already inconvenient.

So the first rule for new owners is to stop holding the MacBook by the screen and learn to open it by the notch or the centre of the lid. Second, do not leave a pen, earphone or cable on the keyboard: a closed lid with a touchscreen forgives this even less than before. And third: the keyboard cover you love so much may leave marks on the lower part of the screen. Now that lower part is also touch-sensitive.

If an impact has already happened, estimate the scale of the disaster yourself. A small crack in the corner with no spots and no loss of touch response — a chance to live until a convenient moment for repair. But stripes, ripples, dead zones or "ghost" presses are a signal not to delay: the flex cables and controllers of the touchscreen do not like working in emergency mode, and it will only get worse.

The finale is simple: the new MacBook Pro is a wonderful machine, but now it is an expensive sandwich of glass and sensor. One awkward move — and the repair budget moves into the category of "we were planning a holiday, not this". Our workshop is ready for such cases — we have diagnostics and skilled hands for the new unit. Drop in, we will sort it out, just do not carry the laptop in one hand by the corner of the screen.
